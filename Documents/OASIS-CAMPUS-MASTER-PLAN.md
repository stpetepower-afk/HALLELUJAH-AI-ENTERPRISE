# Hallelujah ONE™ Oasis Campus — Master Plan

**Status:** Draft — pre-site-control, pre-entity-formation, pre-financing.
**Starting point:** A single-family home slated for teardown and rebuild as a fourplex. This is Phase 1. Nothing below Phase 1 has funding, zoning approval, or a legal entity behind it yet — this document is a planning tool, not a record of commitments made.

---

## 1. Strategic Position

The fourplex is not the campus — it's the proof point. The plan is phased so that each stage has to earn the next one with real, documented outcomes, not projected ones.

| Phase | Scope | Target Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Fourplex Pilot | Teardown/rebuild of the existing single-family lot into a 4-unit building; up to ~16 residents depending on household size | Years 1–2 |
| 2 — Micro-Campus | 4–20 housing units + shared amenities on additional site(s), full HEOS integration | Years 3–4 |
| 3 — Regional Campus | 50–100 units + community hub, HEOS Command Center | Years 5–7 |
| 4 — National Replication | Franchise/affiliate model for other CDCs to adopt | Years 8–10 |

Advancing from one phase to the next should require: documented resident outcomes from the prior phase, a signed financing commitment, and site control for the next phase. Do not schedule Phase 2 activity until Phase 1 has real occupants and real data.

## 2. Phase 1 — Fourplex Pilot

### Target household types
Four units, each intended for a different household situation so the pilot can test the model across cases:

- **Grandfamily housing** — a grandparent raising grandchildren; support = financial coaching, childcare navigation, health navigation
- **Veteran transition housing** — a veteran + family moving toward stability; support = VA benefits navigation, employment, benefits enrollment
- **Workforce family housing** — a working family building savings; support = career development, savings coaching, homeownership prep
- **Reentry/stabilization housing** — an individual rebuilding after a financial or justice-system setback; support = credit recovery, employment, financial literacy

### What "success" means for Phase 1
Track before/after per household, not aggregate marketing numbers:

- Housing stability (still housed at 12/24 months)
- Financial readiness score change (however HEOS ultimately measures this — see open question below)
- Employment status change
- A documented case file per household with coach notes and dated milestones

Do not publish participant counts or outcome numbers until Phase 1 actually has participants. Every metric in this document past this point is a **target**, not a result.

## 3. Governance Structure (proposed)

```
Hallelujah Ministries         — mission, programs
        ↓
Hallelujah ONE CDC            — asset ownership (needs to be formed — see open questions)
        ↓
Property Management Entity    — day-to-day operations
        ↓
HEOS Platform                 — case management / reporting (in active development, see repo)
        ↓
Partners                      — CDFI, energy, healthcare, technology (see §5 — none confirmed yet)
```

The separation matters mainly for liability and grant eligibility: most CDFI and foundation funders want to lend/grant to a dedicated nonprofit or CDC, not directly to a ministry, and separating asset ownership from ministry operations protects both if something goes wrong on either side. None of these entities exist yet — entity formation is a Day 1–30 task (§6).

## 4. Capital Stack (target sources, not secured funding)

| Asset | Target funding types |
|---|---|
| Housing (the fourplex itself) | CDFI acquisition/construction loan, possibly LIHTC or CDBG if the project qualifies at larger scale, private/mission investment |
| Energy infrastructure | Green bonds, utility efficiency programs, equipment partnerships (aspirational — see §5) |
| Technology/education | Foundation grants, corporate in-kind partnerships (aspirational — see §5) |
| Workforce development | State workforce development grants, employer partnerships |
| Wellness | Health-foundation grants |

**Real, verified financing options for a Florida fourplex specifically, worth researching first:**

- **Florida Community Loan Fund (FCLF)** — a real, established statewide CDFI (founded 1994) that finances nonprofit and mission-aligned projects across Florida, including housing. [fclf.org](https://fclf.org)
- **Florida's Live Local Act (SB 102)**, signed March 2023, effective July 1, 2023 — a real, currently active state law that streamlines approval and provides funding mechanisms for affordable/workforce housing. Worth confirming with a land-use attorney whether a 4-unit infill project qualifies for its administrative-approval and funding provisions. [Florida Senate — SB 102](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2023/102)
- **Pinellas County SHIP (State Housing Initiatives Partnership) program** — a real, active county-level program funding new construction, rehab, and gap financing for affordable housing, if the property is in Pinellas County. [Pinellas County SHIP program](https://pinellas.gov/programs/florida-state-housing-initiatives-partnership-program/)

These are real programs, verified today — but eligibility depends on the specific property, its zoning, and its location, which none of us have confirmed yet.

## 6. First 90 Days — Execution Checklist

**Days 1–30 — Foundation**
- [ ] Confirm the property address and current zoning classification
- [ ] Confirm whether a teardown/rebuild to 4 units is allowed as-of-right, or requires a variance/rezoning (this is the single biggest schedule risk — check before anything else)
- [ ] Decide on and begin forming the legal entity (nonprofit CDC vs. LLC vs. fiscal sponsorship under Hallelujah Ministries initially)
- [ ] Build a real, numbers-based pro forma: acquisition cost (if not already owned), demolition cost, construction cost per unit, financing terms, operating budget
- [ ] Identify 2–3 specific CDFI contacts (start with Florida Community Loan Fund) and request an intro call

**Days 31–60 — Validation**
- [ ] Get a real architectural/construction cost estimate for a 4-unit build on the existing lot
- [ ] Get a preliminary energy/utility design and cost estimate (solar/storage optional cost adder, not assumed)
- [ ] Finalize the operating budget, including coach staffing cost — someone has to actually run the case-management side
- [ ] Begin partnership outreach (only after the pro forma exists — don't pitch partners on a project with no numbers)
- [ ] Define the actual resident outcome measurement approach (what score, measured how, by whom)

**Days 61–90 — Funding Readiness**
- [ ] Finish an investor/lender package built on real numbers from Days 1–60
- [ ] Submit to Florida Community Loan Fund and/or the relevant county SHIP office
- [ ] Submit any applicable grant applications
- [ ] Have partnership outreach materials ready to send (not sent automatically — review and send yourself)
- [ ] Lock a realistic construction start date

## 7. Open Questions (answer these before treating any of the above as a real plan)

1. **Where is the property?** County/zoning determines almost everything else (Live Local Act eligibility, SHIP office, applicable CDC/CDFI contacts).
2. **Do you currently own the home outright, or is there a mortgage/lien to address before teardown?**
3. **What's the realistic construction budget, and is there any capital committed yet (personal, ministry, or otherwise) versus 100% dependent on outside financing?**
4. **Who is going to be the case coach / program operator day-to-day?** The HEOS platform tracks cases; it doesn't replace a human coach.
5. **What legal entity structure do you want**, and do you have (or need) an attorney for the CDC formation?

## 8. Relationship to the HEOS Codebase

The HEOS platform (this repo) is the case-management and reporting layer referenced in §3 and the measurement framework in §2. Those are real, buildable next steps once there's a concrete need driving them (e.g., once Phase 1 has actual households to track).
